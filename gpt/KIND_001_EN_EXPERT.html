Risk Brief

Hormuz 2026: anatomy of a shock

From the outbreak of war to the IEA's August report — six months in which volumes collapsed before prices did.

DATATimeline (2026)
Late Feb
War with Iran breaks out. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is effectively blocked. [S4]
Mar 2–3
Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG halts; force majeure declared on the 3rd. [S2][S4]
Mar 11
IEA members agree a 400 mb collective emergency stock release — the largest in IEA history. [S2]
Mar 18
Ras Laffan struck; about 17% of Qatar's LNG capacity lost. [S4]
March
Global oil supply falls 10.1 mb/d. Brent rises about 65% in the month — the largest monthly gain on record. [S3]
Mar 31
Brent peaks at about $118 a barrel. [S4]
Apr 8
Ceasefire. But traffic through the strait stays well below pre-war levels. [S4]
Early July
Strait effectively closed again. As of July, 8.3 mb/d of Gulf output is still shut in. [S1]
End-July
North Sea Dated (spot benchmark) closes at $96.80 — up $25.67 over July. About $92 as of August 12. [S1]
Aug 12
IEA: 2026 world oil demand projected at −1.6 mb/d. Global stocks down 410 mb from late Feb to July. [S1]
!

KIND VIEW Editorial judgment

This crisis is a ‘volume shock’, not a ‘price shock’.

The IEA called this the largest supply disruption in the history of the oil market. Stock releases buy time; they do not create barrels. The global stock draw from late February to July (410 mb) is similar to the March collective release (400 mb) — meaning a cushion the size of the emergency release has already been used.

KIND VIEW Editorial judgment

Bypass capacity is a ‘cushion’, not a ‘replacement’.

The bypass is open only to Saudi and UAE barrels. Of Korea's crude sources, Iraq (10.4%) and Kuwait (8.5%) — about 19% combined — have almost no alternative outside the strait. For Korea, the crisis is less about ‘price’ than about ‘reallocating volumes by source’.

KIND VIEWAssessment criteria
  • Security — does it shrink the physical supply gap within six months?
  • Sustainability — does it move away from, or toward, the 2030 reduction path?
  • Cost — who pays, and when (households / industry / public companies / budget)?

Measures are listed per [S6][S15]; the ▲▼◆ ratings are KIND's editorial judgment.

!

KIND VIEW Editorial judgment

Crisis response does not ‘solve’ the trilemma — it ‘defers’ it.

The carbon cost of lifting the coal cap, the question of who absorbs refiners' losses under the price ceiling, and the cost of restocking at high prices are all bills due after 2027. The quality of the next six months of policy will be judged by who gets those bills, when, and how.

KIND VIEW Editorial judgment

The bottleneck is the grid, not generation.

Grid investment was 40 cents per dollar of generation. Even as grid spending rises to $550bn in 2026, transmission constraints and connection queues are likely to last for years. In Korea too, competition for siting among nuclear, renewables and data centers ultimately comes down to allocating grid capacity.

Gas Market

Gas: short-term shortage, medium-term glut

2026 is tight because of Qatar's losses, but from 2027 large new liquefaction capacity enters the market.

ItemNow (2026)Structure 2027–2030
SupplyAbout 17% of Qatar's LNG capacity lost; Gulf LNG shipping disrupted [S4][S2]About 300 bcm of new liquefaction capacity; net of declining legacy plants, +250 bcm supply [S12]
DemandDemand destruction and fuel switching under high pricesAbout 1.5% a year, +380 bcm (about 9%) by 2030, roughly half of the increase in Asia [S12]
PriceAsian spot prices up more than 140% [S4]Downward pressure from rising supply — KIND judgment
KoreaLNG stocks 52 days [S5]Window to renegotiate long-term contracts and destination flexibility — KIND judgment
KIND VIEWContract strategy checklist
  • Volume — secure crisis volumes with short- and medium-term contracts; negotiate 10-year-plus deals in light of supply growth after 2027.
  • Price index — spread exposure across oil-linked, Henry Hub-linked and hybrid pricing to limit exposure to any single shock.
  • Flexibility — easing destination restrictions and securing resale rights can reduce losses in a glut.
  • Sources — raise the share from sources outside the strait, such as the US (9.4%), Malaysia (16.1%) and Australia.
!

KIND VIEW Editorial judgment

Don't sign 10-year contracts out of today's fear.

With large new supply on the way, long-term contracts locked in at crisis prices become a structural cost burden. Secure volume, but put flexibility in the price terms ahead of it.
