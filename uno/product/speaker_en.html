Transverse Wave
Speaker

UNO SYSTEM|TRANSVERSE WAVE SPEAKER

A new type of ultra-low-power speaker built from an oscillator and a diaphragm. Unlike longitudinal-wave designs that push air back and forth, the diaphragm oscillates side to side or up and down, propagating sound as a transverse wave. Attenuation with distance is low and there is no echo, so speech stays intelligible even in noisy environments.

// System Configuration

System Configuration

STEP 01
🔊
Conventional Speaker (Longitudinal Wave)
A longitudinal-wave design that pushes air back and forth. Wave interference is significant and attenuation with distance is high, so intelligibility drops in noisy environments.
  • Compresses and expands air back and forth
  • Significant wave interference
  • High attenuation with distance
  • Echo occurs

STEP 02
📡
Transverse Wave Speaker (Transverse Wave)
A transverse-wave design in which the diaphragm oscillates sideways to move air particles. Interference is low and attenuation is low, so sound carries clearly over long distances. Bell crickets and bells are classic transverse-wave sound sources.
  • Diaphragm oscillates side to side / up and down
  • Little wave interference
  • Low attenuation with distance
  • No echo (echo-free)
  • Sound source similar to a bell cricket or a bell

STEP 03
📋
Patented Structure
An ultra-low-power oscillator-plus-diaphragm structure based on registered patent KR10-1273442 (June 4, 2013). The diaphragm is fastened so that it flexes at the upper and lower sides of the housing and is secured with a square rubber frame.
  • Patent holder: UNO SYSTEM Co., Ltd. / Inventor: Cha Ju-heon
  • Filed Dec 1, 2011 · Published Jun 11, 2013
  • IPC H04R 7/02 · H04R 1/20
  • Reinforcing plate protects the outside of the board · echo-free
