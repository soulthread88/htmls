📌 Editorial Disclosure
This article is a public-interest commentary based on U.S. State Department official reports, V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) annual reports, credible international reporting from Reuters, BBC, The Guardian, and Foreign Affairs, and official government announcements from multiple countries. It carries no intent to support or disparage any political party or individual. Its purpose is to promote the public's right to know regarding Korea's economic sovereignty and democratic health. Facts and analytical interpretation are clearly distinguished throughout.

In EP.05, we documented how Chinese capital has penetrated deep into Korea's gaming, finance, and media industries. Now in EP.06, we look outward — why are the United States and the United Kingdom issuing unprecedented warnings directed at Korea? And how did the nations that refused to capitulate to China's massive economic retaliation ultimately prevail? Their hard-won lessons illuminate the path Korea must consider walking.

01The Unprecedented Warning Signals the U.S. & U.K. Are Sending Korea

🔍 Top searches right now: "US warning Korea" · "Korea democracy decline" · "V-Dem Korea 2025" · "Finlandization Korea"

For an ally to openly warn another ally is highly unusual by diplomatic convention. Yet between 2025 and 2026, the United States and international democracy watchdog institutions began publicly voicing concern about the state of the rule of law and democratic standards in Korea.

U.S. State Department Joint Strategic Plan (FY 2026–2030, released January 2026)

The U.S. State Department's strategic planning document formally noted that certain legislative movements in Korea could restrict freedom of expression and undermine a fair competitive environment for foreign businesses, signaling that diplomatic tools would be employed if necessary. Such explicit public language directed at an ally is considered highly extraordinary.

— U.S. State Department Official Release, January 2026 / Reported by Chosun Ilbo & Yonhap News —

The American warning is not mere diplomatic rhetoric. The bedrock of the U.S.-Korea alliance is not military cooperation alone — it rests on shared democratic values. Should Korea be perceived as departing from that value foundation, the very credibility of the alliance could be shaken.

V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy Institute) Annual Democracy Report 2025–2026

The V-Dem Institute at the University of Gothenburg, Sweden, measures the democratic health of 179 countries annually. In its 2025–2026 report, Korea showed a declining trend in indicators related to legislative power concentration and judicial independence, and has been flagged under close observation in those metrics.

— V-Dem Institute Annual Democracy Report, 2025/2026 — v-dem.net
KEY ALERT · Finlandization Warning
Finlandization The Cold War phenomenon where Finland constrained its own foreign policy to avoid Soviet displeasure — now being applied as a warning about Korea

Former U.S. Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby warned: "If Korea moves in the direction of yielding to China's political and diplomatic demands because of economic dependency, this is an act that undermines the very effectiveness of the U.S.-Korea alliance." This does not mean voluntary neutrality — it means de facto surrender of sovereignty. — Foreign Affairs, 2025 —

📌 Balanced Perspectives

The pragmatic balance argument: Given Korea's geographic proximity to China, pursuing a practical middle-ground diplomacy between the U.S. and China may be a realistic choice. Fully antagonizing China — Korea's largest trading partner — could deal serious damage to the Korean economy.

The sovereignty-first argument: Conversely, diluting alliance value under the banner of "balance" and gradually acquiescing to a threatening great power is the textbook pattern of Finlandization. Surrendering security and data sovereignty for short-term economic gain ultimately carries a far heavier long-term price.

02Australia — Three Years of Brutal Retaliation, Then Victory

🔍 Top searches: "Australia China trade retaliation" · "Australia China trade war" · "Australia Huawei ban"

Of all the nations that stood against China's economic coercion, Australia's story is the most dramatic tale of ultimate victory. China's punishment was severe — but Australia refused to capitulate, and came back stronger.

🔬
April 2020
Australia Calls for Independent COVID-19 Origin Investigation
The Australian government publicly demanded an independent international inquiry into the origins of COVID-19. China deemed this interference in internal affairs and immediately moved to retaliate.
🚫
2020–2021
China's Sweeping Economic Retaliation — 14 Sectors Hit Simultaneously
China imposed an 80.5% tariff on Australian barley and up to 218% tariffs on wine, while banning or restricting imports of beef, coal, timber, copper, sugar, cotton, and seafood. Approximately AUD 20 billion in Australian exports were affected across the full retaliation period. — Source: Australian Strategic Policy Institute (ASPI), 2021
💪
2021–2022
Australia's Choice — Diversification Instead of Capitulation
Rather than surrendering, Australia pivoted aggressively to diversify export markets. Trade flows shifted toward India, Southeast Asia, and Europe. Simultaneously, Australia strengthened its security autonomy through the QUAD partnership and the AUKUS defense pact.
✅
2023–2024
China Blinks First — Australia's Victory
Remarkably, it was China that first sought normalization. Tariffs on Australian wine and beef were progressively lifted. By that point, Australian exporters had already opened new markets. — Source: Reuters, "Australia-China trade normalization," March 2024
Australian Strategic Policy Institute (ASPI) — Core Lesson

ASPI analyzed the Australian case and concluded: "China's economic retaliation is painful, but the nation that held its principles ultimately secured the stronger position. Capitulation only invites larger demands."

— Australian Strategic Policy Institute (ASPI), 2023 / aspi.org.au

03Lithuania — How a Nation of 2.8 Million Defied China

🔍 Top searches: "Lithuania Taiwan representative office" · "Lithuania Taiwan China" · "Lithuania China diplomatic break"

In 2021, Lithuania — a nation of just 2.8 million people — made one of the most audacious diplomatic decisions in the world: it allowed a "Taiwanese Representative Office" to open in its capital, Vilnius. China immediately recalled its ambassador and branded Lithuania a violator of the One China Principle.

China's Retaliation — Complete Trade Blockade
€2.1B Lithuania's annual export volume to China — effectively 100% blocked (2021–2022)

China removed Lithuania from its customs system in an extreme measure that amounted to a de facto trade siege. German and other EU companies with Lithuanian components in their supply chains were also pressured, facing threats of lost access to the Chinese market. — Source: Reuters, "China blocks Lithuania imports," December 2021

Yet Lithuania did not yield. The EU responded collectively, and proceedings were initiated at the European Court of Justice (ECJ). Taiwan established an economic cooperation fund with Lithuania, and the United States pledged trade support.

Lithuanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs — Official Position (2022)

The Lithuanian government declared: "We do not capitulate to economic intimidation. If a small country fails to uphold its principles, large countries have no reason to uphold theirs. Our decision is founded on the values of democracy and human rights — and those values are not negotiable."

— Lithuanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Official Statement 2022 / BBC News —

04Lessons from Canada & the U.K. — Huawei Bans and Investment Screening

🔍 Top searches: "Canada Huawei ban" · "UK NSI Act" · "Five Eyes China"

Through the Huawei question, Canada and the United Kingdom demonstrated in practice that telecommunications infrastructure sovereignty is security sovereignty. Both nations' decisions were long-term security investments made at the cost of short-term economic pain.

The arrest of Huawei's CFO triggered fierce Chinese retaliation (the detention of two Canadians in what critics called "hostage diplomacy"). Yet Canada ultimately completed the Huawei ban, and China was forced to release the detained Canadians. — Source: CBC News, 2021

U.K. · National Security and Investment Act (NSI Act) — In Force 2022

The U.K. enacted the NSI Act in 2022, mandating government notification for foreign acquisitions of 25% or more equity in 17 strategic sectors including semiconductors, AI, defense, and telecommunications. Under this law, the U.K. government forced the reversal of a Chinese acquisition of Newport Wafer Fab, a British semiconductor company. This is a direct legislative model Korea should reference.

— UK Department for Business, Energy & Industrial Strategy, Official Release 2022 / gov.uk
📌 Implications for Korea

Legislative gap: Korea currently conducts foreign investment screening only in select areas such as defense and nuclear energy. For sectors like gaming, finance, media, and digital platforms — where Chinese capital has already penetrated deeply — a meaningful screening framework effectively does not exist.

Urgency: The U.K. created its NSI Act after Chinese capital had already entered. Korea faces the same reality, but failing to build legal safeguards now leaves no mechanism to prevent further penetration.

05Where Does Korea Stand? — THAAD's Lessons & Today's Choice

🔍 Top searches: "THAAD retaliation Korea ban" · "Korea China economic dependency" · "Clive Hamilton Silent Invasion"

In 2017, Korea experienced China's THAAD retaliation — a tourism ban, the so-called "Korean Wave ban" (한한령), and pressure on Lotte Mart. Korea's response at the time differed sharply from that of Australia or Lithuania: it amounted to a de facto diplomatic concession in the form of the "Three No's" pledge.

🇰🇷
Korea — THAAD Case (2017)
Cautionary Failure
In response to China's economic retaliation (tourism ban, Korean Wave ban), Korea effectively accepted the "Three No's" pledge — no additional THAAD deployments, no joining the U.S. missile defense system, no trilateral military alliance with the U.S. and Japan. Yet Chinese retaliation was never fully lifted, and Korea's security autonomy was damaged.
🇦🇺
Australia — COVID Inquiry Demand (2020)
Victory on Principle
Australia paid the severe price of AUD 20 billion in blocked exports — yet refused to yield. Three years later, China was the one to extend an olive branch, and Australian exporters had already developed new markets. It proved that "principled refusal produces better outcomes."
🇱🇹
Lithuania — Taiwan Office (2021)
Values Diplomacy Win
With the collective response of the EU and solidarity from Taiwan and the U.S., Lithuania withstood what amounted to a full trade blockade. A nation of 2.8 million sent the world a message: "Democratic values are non-negotiable."

Both the Australian Strategic Policy Institute (ASPI) and the UK's Chatham House have observed that Korea has repeatedly fallen into a "concession → larger demands" cycle in its relationship with China. They recommend that breaking this pattern requires setting clear principles at an earlier stage and coordinating responses with allies.

Clive Hamilton, Silent Invasion: China's Influence in Australia (2018)

Australian political scientist Clive Hamilton documented in detail how China penetrates the political, media, academic, and business spheres of democratic nations with capital, distorting decision-making structures. Even without direct orders from Beijing, "collaborators" within these societies move of their own accord in directions favorable to China. Concerns have been raised that Korea's situation lies on precisely this analytical continuum.

— Clive Hamilton, Silent Invasion: China's Influence in Australia, Hardie Grant Books, 2018 —

🛡️ What Korea Must Do Now
Five action principles revealed by Western warnings and successful case studies
LEGISLATIVE ACTION
📜
Enact a Korean NSI Act
Like the U.K.'s National Security and Investment Act, Korea urgently needs legislation mandating foreign investment screening in gaming, finance, platforms, and telecommunications.
ALLIANCE BUILDING
🤝
Clarify Values-Based Alliances
Deepen solidarity with the U.S., EU, and Japan on shared democratic values. Chinese retaliation is most devastating when faced alone — collective response is the answer.
SUPPLY CHAIN STRATEGY
🔄
Diversify Exports & Sourcing
Like Australia, Korea must reduce China dependency and open markets in India, Southeast Asia, and Europe. Build alternative routes before retaliation arrives.
DATA SOVEREIGNTY
🔐
Enact Data Localization Law
Legislation prohibiting the offshore transfer of domestic user data must be enacted without delay, establishing data localization as a legal baseline.
CIVIC OVERSIGHT
👁️
Monitor Chinese Capital Influence
Civil society must continuously monitor and bring to public discourse the indirect influence of Chinese capital in media, politics, and academia.
