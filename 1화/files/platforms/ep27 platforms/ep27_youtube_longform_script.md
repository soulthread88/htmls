### [SEGMENT 1 — HOW FALL DETECTION WORKS — 1:20–2:45]

**[ON SCREEN: Simple animation of watch sensors]**

**JAKE:**
"First — how does a smartwatch actually know you've fallen?

Your Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch has two sensors built in:
An accelerometer — which measures sudden changes in speed and direction.
And a gyroscope — which measures rotation and body orientation.

When these two sensors detect the pattern of a fall — a sudden downward impact followed by a period of no movement — the watch triggers fall detection.

Here's exactly what happens next:
Step one: The watch vibrates strongly and makes a loud sound.
Step two: A message appears on screen: 'It looks like you've had a hard fall.'
Step three: A 60-second countdown begins.
Step four: If you tap 'I'm OK' — the alarm cancels. No emergency call.
Step five: If you DON'T respond in 60 seconds — the watch automatically calls 911.
And step six: It sends your GPS location and a text alert to your registered emergency contacts.

Grandpa Bob, what do you think?"

"So the watch calls for help even if I'm unconscious and can't press anything?"

**JAKE:**

### [SEGMENT 2 — APPLE WATCH SETUP — 2:45–4:30]

**[ON SCREEN: iPhone and Apple Watch screen recordings, step by step]**

**JAKE:**
"Let's set this up on Apple Watch first — supported on Series 4 and all newer models.

Step One: On your iPhone, open the Watch app. It has a little watch face as its icon.
Step Two: Tap 'Emergency SOS' in the menu list.
Step Three: Find 'Fall Detection' — and toggle it ON. That's it. Done.

One important note: If you're under 55 years old, Apple Watch only activates fall detection automatically during workouts. But if you're 55 or older, it's always on. And you can manually turn it on for any age in that Emergency SOS menu.

Step Four — and this is critical — scroll down in Emergency SOS to 'Emergency Contacts.'
Tap 'Add Emergency Contact.'
Choose from your contacts — and assign a relationship label like 'son,' 'daughter,' 'neighbor.'

These people will receive an automatic text message with your location when 911 is called.

[PAUSE for emphasis]

Please don't skip that step. The watch calling 911 is powerful. But your family also knowing — immediately — is what saves minutes. And minutes matter after a fall."

### [SEGMENT 3 — GALAXY WATCH SETUP — 4:30–6:00]

**[ON SCREEN: Galaxy Watch and Samsung Health app screen recordings]**

**JAKE:**
"Now for Galaxy Watch — the process is slightly different but just as simple.

Step One: On your Galaxy Watch, open the Samsung Health app directly on the watch face.
OR — open Samsung Health on your smartphone and sync the settings.
Step Two: Go to Settings — look for the gear icon.
Step Three: Find 'Fall Detection' — toggle it ON.

Now for emergency contacts on Galaxy Watch:
Open Samsung Health on your phone.
Tap your profile icon at the top right.
Select 'Emergency Contacts.'
Add up to four contacts — and double-check their phone numbers are correct.

Galaxy Watch also has a dedicated SOS button feature — press and hold the home button for 3 seconds to manually trigger an emergency call even without a fall. Show your parent where that button is. Practice it together."

### [SEGMENT 4 — EMERGENCY CONTACTS — THE MOST IMPORTANT STEP — 6:00–7:15]

**[ON SCREEN: Side-by-side iPhone and Galaxy Watch emergency contact setup]**

**JAKE:**
"I want to stop here and be very clear about emergency contacts — because this is the step most families skip, and it's the one that matters most.

When 911 is called by the watch:
The ambulance comes — yes.
But does your family know?
Does your neighbor know?
Does anyone know to meet the ambulance at the hospital?

Emergency contacts solve this. The moment the watch calls 911, every person on that list receives an automatic text message — right now, your GPS location, and a note that emergency services have been contacted.

My recommendation: Register THREE contacts.
Contact 1: Nearest family member
Contact 2: A close neighbor who can physically check on your parent
Contact 3: A secondary family member in a different location

And then — call each of those contacts and tell them: 'You're on my emergency list. If you get a text from my watch, please go check on me immediately.'

### [SEGMENT 5 — FALSE ALARMS: HOW TO CANCEL — 7:15–8:30]

**[ON SCREEN: Demo of the cancel process on Apple Watch]**

**JAKE:**
"Let's talk about false alarms — because they happen, and your parent needs to know what to do.

The most common false alarm scenario: bending down to pick something up, a sudden sit-down in a chair, or bumping the watch against a hard surface.

When a false alarm triggers:
You'll hear a loud alert and feel a strong vibration.
The screen shows the 60-second countdown.

To cancel: Press the Digital Crown — the round button on the side of Apple Watch — firmly. The alert cancels immediately. No call is made.

For Galaxy Watch: Press the home button to cancel.

Here's my strong advice — practice this with your parent.
Sit down together.
Explain: 'If your watch ever makes this sound and shows a countdown, press THIS button.'
Point to the button. Have them press it once with their finger.
That 10-second practice session could prevent an unnecessary 911 dispatch.

One more tip: After a false alarm, Samsung Health will ask you to confirm whether a fall actually occurred. Always answer honestly — this helps the AI improve its detection accuracy over time."

### [SEGMENT 6 — BONUS SAFETY FEATURES — 8:30–10:00]

**[ON SCREEN: Split screen of multiple watch health features]**

**JAKE:**
"Fall detection is the headliner — but your smartwatch does so much more to keep your parent safe.

Feature Two: Real-Time Location Sharing.
On iPhone: Open Find My → Share My Location with family members. They can see exactly where your parent is at any time — right on a map.
On Galaxy Watch: Samsung's SmartThings Find works the same way.

Feature Three: Irregular Heartbeat Detection.
Apple Watch Series 4 and later can detect atrial fibrillation — AFib — an irregular heart rhythm that significantly increases stroke risk.
Galaxy Watch 5 and later includes the same capability.
If AFib is detected, the watch sends an alert and can generate an ECG report to share with your doctor.

Important legal note: These features are not FDA-cleared diagnostic tools for AFib in all contexts. A physician must evaluate any alerts. But as a screening signal — they've genuinely saved lives.

Feature Four: Blood Oxygen Monitoring.
Available on Apple Watch Series 6 and later, and Galaxy Watch 4 and later.
Low blood oxygen — below 90% — can signal respiratory problems, heart issues, or at high altitude, altitude sickness.
The watch alerts you when readings drop into concerning ranges.
