SCADA / BACnet / Modbus Support
Interworks flexibly with a wide range of upper-level supervisory systems. RS-485 and TCP/IP interfaces come as standard, so integration with existing infrastructure is quick and direct connection is possible without protocol conversion equipment. Broad support for industrial standards such as SCADA, BACnet, and Modbus means data exchange with building automation systems (BAS) or a central control server is unrestricted. Thanks to the open communication architecture, future system expansion or the addition of third-party equipment is easy to accommodate.

02 · Wireless Communication Device

Wireless Communication Device

🔋
Low-Power Design
An ultra-low-power circuit design that allows long battery life minimizes the maintenance cycle after installation. The device runs independently without dedicated power wiring, so it installs easily in areas where cabling is difficult or in retrofits of existing buildings. Low-power sleep modes and event-driven communication curb battery drain while still delivering fire signals without delay. By greatly reducing power infrastructure cost and installation effort, it lowers total cost of ownership (TCO).
+

Low-power wireless communication device
🔗
Panel Integration
Communicates wirelessly and directly with the auxiliary control panel to relay fire signals instantly. A reliable two-way wireless link is maintained without any cabling, and panel-side acknowledgment (ACK) eliminates dropped signals. Continuous self-diagnosis of link status notifies administrators the moment a fault occurs, so no monitoring gap arises. Many wireless devices can be grouped under a single auxiliary control panel, covering large areas economically.

Fiber-Optic Detector
A rate-of-rise distributed detector that measures temperature continuously over runs of several kilometers with a single strand of optical cable. Raman backscattering captures the temperature profile of the entire cable in real time and pinpoints hot spots to within meters. Immune to electromagnetic interference and suitable for explosion-proof environments, it is ideal for harsh sites such as tunnels, utility tunnels, and underground ducts. It is a proven detection solution with KFI type approvals DET24-74 and DET26-9.

Fiber-Optic Detector (DET24-74 / DET26-9)
🔴
R-Type FACP
An integrated R-type control panel that converts the analog temperature and fire information gathered by the fiber-optic detector into digital signals. With DTS (distributed temperature sensing) built into the panel, the entire cable run is monitored on one screen and an abnormal temperature triggers an alarm together with its location. 6 kV surge protection comes as standard to shield the circuitry from lightning and induced surges. As a type-approved FACP25-66 product, it operates reliably even in harsh environments such as extra-high-voltage utility tunnels.

📡
TCP/IP Network
The communication layer that carries digitized fire information from the R-type control panel to upper-level systems. Support for RS-485 and Ethernet (TCP/IP) means it slots straight into existing network infrastructure, and standard protocols allow free data exchange between equipment from different vendors. A redundant configuration keeps communication alive even if one path fails. It serves as a stable backbone that delivers real-time data without delay, even on large sites.

🖥️
Integrated Fire Monitoring
Fire information delivered over the network is supervised in real time by the integrated fire monitoring auxiliary control panel and the GIS system. The fire location is visualized as coordinates on a map, so administrators can see at a glance what is happening and where. Zone and equipment details are shown alongside, allowing fast initial assessment and response orders. Multiple facilities are monitored from a single control screen, completing an integrated safety management framework.

Fire Department Integrated Control
Finally, a direct link to the 119 fire department integrated control center shares the fire location and situation immediately. Automatic notification shortens the time from report to dispatch, and crews respond already holding on-site information, improving early suppression. Real-time data sharing with the responsible fire crews supports a coordinated command structure even during large-scale emergencies. This is the final supervisory stage, connecting everything from detection to fire response into a single flow.
