Seoul · National Crisis Management Center

The Alert Lights Come On

Well past midnight, the fluorescents in the underground bunker snapped on all at once. On the wall-sized screen, red contacts kept multiplying across the Strait of Hormuz. The National Security Director stood there with the report in his hand and, for a long moment, couldn't make himself read it out loud.

"How many of our ships are in that water right now?" His question hung in the air. The staffer answering him had to steady his own hand before he could get the number out. Seventeen. Three of them had already gone dark — their last transmission was a course change nobody in this room had ordered.

Strait of Hormuz · Oil Tanker M/V Hanbit

Dead in the Water

Tracer fire streaked red across Hanbit's deck. The captain stood pressed to the wheelhouse glass, staring into the dark. The engines had been silent for three hours. The radio gave back nothing but static.

"Home office, come in." He sent the call out again — the umpteenth time now, he'd lost count — and got nothing back. Below deck, the crew sat crowded in a narrow passage in their life vests. Nobody said a word.

Busan · ROK Fleet Command

Get Underway

Standing in front of the operations board, Captain Park read the order twice. ROKS Sejong the Great — underway immediately. The mission was simple enough on paper: secure the safety of Korean-flagged vessels near Hormuz. Nobody in that room needed reminding that the water in question was already a battlefield.

"Engines ready. We can get underway inside thirty minutes," his executive officer reported. Park gave a short nod. Out on the bridge wing, the cold night air hit his face. He closed his eyes for a moment — then opened them again.

A City That Can't Sleep

A Blinking Light

Strait of Hormuz · First Light

The Night the Wall Fell

By the time Sejong the Great came alongside Hanbit, the sky had already started to pale. The unidentified craft that had been closing were gone, scattered back into the dark. Whether they'd been a threat or a false alarm, no one would ever confirm.

Captain Park stood on deck and looked back at the night behind him. In a single day, a head of state was dead, a strait had become a battlefield, and the world had crossed a threshold it wasn't going back over. Something that had looked unshakeable for a long, long time had come down — quietly, in the dark.
