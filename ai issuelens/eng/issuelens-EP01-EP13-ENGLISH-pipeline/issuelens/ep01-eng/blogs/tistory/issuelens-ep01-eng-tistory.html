Temu, AliExpress, Shein, and MINISO are among the most downloaded apps in America right now. But while millions of shoppers chase "impossibly low prices," federal agencies, state attorneys general, and national security experts are raising serious alarms about what these apps do with your personal data — and who can access it.

01What Temu & AliExpress Know About You (It's More Than You Think)

🔍 Top searches right now: "Is Temu safe to use?" · "Does AliExpress steal your data?" · "Should I delete Temu from my phone?" · "Is Shein a spyware app?"

The moment you install Temu, AliExpress, or Shein and enter your payment info, the data collection begins — and it goes far beyond your name and shipping address. Device fingerprinting, location tracking, behavioral profiling, and contact list access are all documented practices. The question isn't whether they collect your data. The question is: what happens to it next?

LIVE ALERT · Texas AG Lawsuit — February 2026
$1M+ Minimum monetary relief sought — Texas v. Temu (Feb. 20, 2026)

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton filed suit against Temu, describing the app as "spyware disguised as a shopping app" — joining AGs from at least four other states. The suit alleges illegal data harvesting, deceptive pricing, and violations of the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Earlier, 21 U.S. attorneys general called for a federal investigation into Temu's ties to the Chinese government.

02Why China's National Intelligence Law Is Every American's Problem

🔍 Top searches: "Can China access Temu data?" · "Is Temu a national security threat?" · "Chinese apps spy on Americans" · "Temu linked to CCP"

This isn't just a privacy issue. It's a structural legal problem. Unlike Western companies bound by frameworks like GDPR or CCPA, Chinese companies face a fundamentally different obligation — one that has national security experts, the FBI, and Congress deeply alarmed.

China National Intelligence Law — Article 7 (2017, currently in force)

All organizations and citizens shall support, assist, and cooperate with national intelligence work in accordance with the law, and shall protect national intelligence work secrets they are aware of.

— Enacted 2017 · Actively enforced —

This means every Chinese company — including Temu's parent PDD Holdings — can legally be compelled to hand over user data to the Chinese government, with no judicial oversight, no notice to users, and no ability to refuse. Your grocery list, your address, your financial habits: all potentially accessible.

03The Double Standard: They Block Our Apps — We Hand Them Our Data

🔍 Top searches: "Why is Temu so cheap?" · "What does Temu do with my information?" · "Is it safe to give Temu your credit card?" · "Temu data breach"

Here's the asymmetry Americans need to understand: Google, Instagram, YouTube, and Facebook are banned inside China. Yet Temu, AliExpress, and TikTok freely operate inside the United States, collecting hundreds of millions of behavioral profiles from American users every single day. Is that a fair trade?

📌 CONTEXT · What the FBI Said in Early 2026

The FBI issued a formal warning that many of the most downloaded apps in America are maintained by foreign companies — particularly those based in China — and that apps with servers in China are subject to laws that enable Chinese government access to user data. The bureau specifically flagged aggressive data collection and malware risk as the two primary threat categories. Its advice: check your permissions, and delete apps you don't trust.

FACT CHECK · FTC Formal Warning — January 2026
3rd Time the FTC warned Temu over "inadequate transparency in cross-border data transfers"

The Federal Trade Commission's latest notice cited Temu's failure to adequately disclose how user data is transferred across borders. Security researchers separately found that Temu's parent company's sister app, Pinduoduo, was removed from the Google Play Store after malware was discovered — and some of those same developers later joined Temu's engineering team.

🛡️ Protect Yourself — 4 Steps Right Now
You can't unring the bell, but you can stop the bleeding
STEP 01
🗑️
Delete the Apps
Remove Temu, AliExpress, Shein, and similar apps. Deleting the app doesn't delete your data from their servers — also submit a formal data deletion request through each app's privacy settings.
STEP 02
🔒
Lock Down Permissions
Go to Settings → Apps and review what permissions each app has. Revoke access to your microphone, camera, contacts, and location for any app that doesn't absolutely need them.
STEP 03
📣
Tell Someone You Know
Share this with your family and friends — especially older relatives who may not know about these risks. Awareness is the first line of defense. Knowledge spreads faster than any app.
STEP 04
🗳️
Contact Your Rep
The U.S. still has no federal data privacy law. Contact your Congressional representatives and demand action. Digital sovereignty is the new frontier of American freedom.
