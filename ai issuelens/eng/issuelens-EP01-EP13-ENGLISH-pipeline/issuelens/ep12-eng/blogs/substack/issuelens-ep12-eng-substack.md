## The Big Picture

Lose the port, lose the nation. In EP.11, we examined how Alibaba, Temu, and Miniso are encircling Korea's consumer ecosystem. EP.12 operates on an entirely different scale. Sri Lanka lost 99 years of sovereign control over a single port. Greece surrendered 67% of its largest harbor to China amid a debt crisis. Africa's Djibouti became home to China's first overseas military base. What guarantee is there that this pattern won't repeat itself in Northeast Asia? Busan — the world's 7th-largest container port — and Incheon, the gateway for Korea-China trade: we trace what would happen if Chinese capital entered South Korea's heartland.

## 🛡️ Action Items

**Legal Protections for Critical Infrastructure**
Designate Busan and Incheon ports as national security assets by law, mandate screening of foreign capital seeking port operating rights or equity stakes, and introduce a Golden Share regime.

**Immediate Port Equipment Security Audit**
Conduct cybersecurity audits of Chinese-manufactured cranes and automated systems (including ZPMC products), and establish response protocols in line with U.S. DoD warnings.

**Diversify Energy Import Routes**
Secure alternative energy corridors to reduce Malacca Strait dependency. Diversify energy supply routes through Australia, Qatar, and Norway, and strengthen strategic energy reserves.

**Strengthen U.S.–Korea Port Security Cooperation**
Build a joint Korea-U.S. port security framework aligned with the U.S. "Strategic Ports Reporting Act" (2025), and leverage QUAD cooperation for broader multilateral maritime security.

**Secure Port Data Sovereignty**
Restrict foreign company participation in port operating IT systems, mandate domestic storage of port operations data, and legislate data residency requirements for all port management systems.
