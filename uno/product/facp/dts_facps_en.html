// DTS Integrated R-Type Fire Alarm Control Panel

DTS-Integrated
R-Type FACP

UNO SYSTEM|MDT-1000F | UFC-OR12

A DTS-equipped fire alarm control panel optimized for surge- and noise-heavy environments such as utility tunnels, communication tunnels, and underground common utility tunnels. With a built-in fiber-optic rate-of-rise distributed detector (DET24-74), it monitors fire and temperature over a span of up to 6 km at 1.0 m resolution and can be interlocked with SCADA and integrated management systems.

Key Features

Distributed Temperature Sensing (DTS)
A Distributed Temperature Sensing (DTS) system developed entirely with domestic technology is built in. Precision optics and signal processing deliver world-class accuracy and resolution.

// Applications

Applications Areas

⚡
Underground Utility Tunnel Fire Monitoring
Underground utility tunnels are densely packed with high-voltage cables, so insulation degradation or an overheating joint can easily lead to fire. Running a fiber-optic distributed detector along the cable trays makes it possible to monitor temperature continuously across the entire run at 1.0 m resolution. Rising-temperature trends are captured before a local hot spot crosses the alarm threshold, so a warning is raised early. Because sensing is purely optical, it is immune to electromagnetic interference even in underground environments with heavy surges and noise. The point of ignition can be pinpointed to within meters, enabling a fast first response and rapid restoration.
🖼
Image / Video
📡
Communication Tunnels · Common Utility Tunnels
Communication tunnels and multi-service utility tunnels carry telecom, power, and water lines together, so fire risk factors are compounded. A single strand of optical cable monitors a run of up to 6 km without gaps, eliminating the need to install many individual spot detectors. Even the smallest temperature changes in an enclosed underground space are detected, so anomalies are flagged before smoke spreads. In long underground sections that are hard to service, the detector itself has no power supply, which keeps the maintenance burden low. Interlocking with SCADA and integrated management systems allows status to be checked remotely in real time.
🖼
Image / Video
🚇
Tunnel Fire Monitoring
Road and rail tunnels are high-risk spaces where a vehicle fire spreads smoke and heat rapidly and can cause mass casualties. Optical cable is run along the tunnel ceiling for the entire length, so a fire at any point is located immediately. Rate-of-rise logic based on the temperature gradient distinguishes vehicle exhaust heat from an actual fire. Interlocking with smoke control and evacuation guidance systems enables automatic control tailored to the fire location. Even in extra-long tunnels, redundant configurations provide uninterrupted continuous monitoring.
🖼
Image / Video
🏭
Power Plant Transmission Line Monitoring
Transmission cables at power plants and substations are prone to local overheating when operated under overload. Optical fiber run along the cable surface measures the temperature profile in real time and identifies abnormally hot sections early. Loads can then be managed so that the cable's allowable current is not exceeded, extending equipment life. Preventive maintenance is triggered before conditions escalate into fire, heading off outages. Measurement data accumulates in the control system for equipment condition diagnostics and predictive maintenance.
🖼
Image / Video
🛢️
Gas & Crude Oil Storage
At facilities storing hazardous materials such as gas and crude oil, even a small fire can escalate into a major explosion, making early detection paramount. Fiber-optic sensing carries no risk of electrical sparks, making it ideal for explosion-proof, hazardous environments. Continuous temperature monitoring around storage tanks and transfer piping catches temperature changes from a leak or an incipient fire immediately. An entire large storage site is monitored as one integrated system, eliminating blind spots. Interlocking with fire suppression systems and emergency shutoff valves enables an automatic protective response.
🖼
Image / Video
🧪
Chemical Plant Piping
Transfer piping at chemical plants handles hot and corrosive substances, so leaks and overheating are a constant risk. Optical cable run along the piping monitors surface temperature in real time across the entire route. A temperature anomaly at a specific point may signal a leak or a runaway reaction, so early warning prevents accidents. Because sensing is optical, it operates reliably even in plant environments with severe electromagnetic interference. Data can be linked to the process control system so that an affected section is isolated and shut down immediately.
🖼
Image / Video
🌊
Pipeline Leak Monitoring
Long-distance pipelines are buried over such long runs that visual inspection is effectively impossible. Optical fiber laid along the pipeline continuously measures the temperature profile over many kilometers. Local temperature changes produced by a fluid leak (the Joule-Thomson effect, among others) are detected, pinpointing the leak location precisely. Shutdown and repair can therefore take place before the situation escalates into a large-scale spill. A single system monitors a vast run without on-site staff, cutting operating costs.
🖼
Image / Video
❄️
Cold Storage & Refrigerated Warehouses
In cold storage and refrigerated warehouses, maintaining temperature is directly tied to product quality and safety. Optical cable run throughout the storage space monitors the temperature profile in real time. A local temperature rise may be a precursor to a cooling equipment failure or a fire, so an alarm is raised at once. Uniform temperature control can be verified across a large warehouse with no blind spots. The accumulated temperature data supports cold-chain quality management and provides evidence for equipment maintenance.
🖼

// How it Works

Operating Principle

01
Normal Monitoring
The DTS launches a 1550 nm laser into the optical cable and analyzes the Raman backscatter to measure the temperature profile along the entire run continuously, on a cycle of 5 seconds to 30 minutes. The panel's normal AC power and normal backup power indicators are lit.
02
Fire / Abnormal Temperature Detection
When a detector exceeds its set temperature, the fire location appears on the LCD and the fire LED lights. The main and zone bells sound, and the same fire alarm is raised when a manual call point is activated.
03
Alarm Silencing and Interlock Control
The main-bell stop and zone-bell stop switches silence alarms individually, and the interlock stop switch disables all interlocked equipment at once. The buzzer stop and reset switches manage panel status.
04
Integrated Management System Interlock
Real-time data and alarms are transmitted to the fire department's integrated monitoring facility and the control room's integrated management system through RS-485, RS-232, SCADA, and Ethernet interfaces.
05
Backup Power Switchover and Reset
When mains power is lost, the panel switches automatically to the Ni-Cd battery and the backup-power fault LED lights. After the fire system is initialized, the auto-reset function returns it to normal status.
06
Line-Fault Monitoring
If the optical cable or a connecting line breaks, the buzzer sounds and the line-fault LED lights. The fault event is stored in the logging device to support maintenance.
